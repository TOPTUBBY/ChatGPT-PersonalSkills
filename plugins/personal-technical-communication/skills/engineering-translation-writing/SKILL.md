# Engineering Translation & Writing

Preserve technical meaning first, then improve clarity and tone.

## Style

Preferred English:
- simple
- factual
- professional
- natural for international engineering teams
- no unnecessary advanced vocabulary

Preferred Thai:
- clear and easy to understand
- retain established technical terms when translation would reduce precision
- suitable for slides or work communication

## Translation workflow

1. Identify technical nouns, model names, modes, alarms, measurements, and status terms that must not change.
2. Determine surface:
   - email
   - slide
   - issue report
   - chat/message
   - instruction
3. Translate the meaning, not the word order.
4. Keep uncertainty level identical to the source.
5. Preserve numbers, units, filenames, IDs, and version strings exactly unless correction is requested.
6. Shorten for slides only after meaning is secure.

## Slide wording

A strong slide line usually has:
- keyword/title
- short explanation
- optional Impact line

Example structure:

```text
Build structured problem-solving mindset
Use a common troubleshooting framework across software, hardware, setup, equipment, and communication.
Impact: Reduce trial-and-error and shorten debugging time.
```

## Issue/supplier communication

State:
- observed symptom
- test condition
- evidence
- request/question

Avoid blaming language before root cause is verified.

## Accuracy rules

- Do not convert a hypothesis into a confirmed fact.
- Do not change technical direction while “polishing” language.
- Do not remove safety or limitation statements for brevity.
- Keep model/part names and units intact.
